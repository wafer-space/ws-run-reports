What is Tiny Tapeout?{{ anchor("tinytapeout") }}

{{ tt("home", "Tiny Tapeout") }} makes it quicker, easier and cheaper than ever to get your own design made on a real chip.

Many designs share one Tiny Tapeout die. Each project gets one or more small {{ tt("specs", "tiles") }}, and a multiplexer on the chip connects the one you select to the pins. You can draw your first design in the browser, with no previous experience, by following the {{ tt("digital_design", "digital design guide") }}, or write it in {{ tt("hdl", "Verilog or another HDL") }}. Tiny Tapeout's templates build and check it on GitHub, and the chip comes on a {{ tt("demoboard", "demo board") }} that selects and drives your design.

Tiny Tapeout also runs {{ tt("workshops", "workshops") }} and supports {{ tt("teaching", "teaching") }} in universities and schools. Browse {{ tt("chips", "every Tiny Tapeout chip") }} and the {{ tt("silicon_proven", "projects proven in silicon") }}, or read the {{ tt("faq", "FAQ") }}.

{% endmacro %} {% macro independent() %}

Tiny Tapeout and wafer.space are independent companies that work together. Tiny Tapeout runs its own shuttles: it takes the projects, builds the die, and sells and supports the chips and boards. wafer.space has the die manufactured as one of the designs on its GF180MCU runs, and these pages are wafer.space's measurements of it as manufactured.

For anything about a Tiny Tapeout project, chip or order, {{ tt("contact", "contact Tiny Tapeout") }}. Tiny Tapeout's {{ tt("partnership", "announcement of the partnership") }} tells how it began.

Not ready to make your own chip?

Tiny Tapeout offers a cheap and easy way to get started. Put a small design on a shared Tiny Tapeout chip, from a few logic gates drawn in your browser to your own Verilog, and get it back on a board you can plug in. When your design outgrows a few tiles, a whole die on a wafer.space run is the next step.
